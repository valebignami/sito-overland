Continuous
coil anodizing.

Anodizing is an electrochemical process that grows a hard, protective oxide layer on aluminium. The layer is part of the metal itself, not a coating on top of it.

Five stages,
one line.

The coil unwinds at one end of the line and is rewound at the other. In between it passes through every bath without stopping, never clamped or racked.

  1. CleaningRemoves oils and residues left from rolling.
  2. EtchingEvens out the surface and sets the base look before anodizing.
  3. AnodizingElectric current grows the oxide layer, 2 to 10 µm as standard, up to 15 µm on request.
  4. ColouringThe shade is fixed inside the pores of the oxide, so it cannot peel or flake.
  5. SealingCloses the pores to lock in colour and corrosion resistance.

Coil or batch.

Both methods anodize aluminium. The difference is how the metal travels through the baths, and it shows in the surface you receive.

Continuous coil

How Overland works

The whole coil moves through all baths on one line.

Batch

Parts or sheets hang from racks and are dipped tank by tank.

Surface

Coil: No contact or hanging marks.

Batch: Contact points where the rack holds the part.

Colour

Coil: Consistent along the coil and between runs.

Batch: Can vary from one batch to the next.

Format

Coil: Coils, sheets and strips, ready for fabrication.

Batch: Finished parts and profiles.

Resources

Coil: Fewer chemicals, less water and energy, less waste.

Batch: More chemicals, water and energy for the same result.

Cost

Coil: An efficient line keeps production costs down, and customers see it in the price.

Batch: Racking and handling each batch add to production costs.
